Squash Ball Detector
Daniel Shubin, Jules Ropars, Lawrence Tan, Lyle 
Deng

---===---
Overview
-
Task: Given an image of a squash court, train a CV model 
to be able to accurately predict the location of the 
ball.
-
Challenge: Unlike other sports, in squash the ball is 
much smaller (only 40mm diameter) and moves much faster. 
This, combined with images also containing players moving 
at high speeds nearby, makes the ball tough to identify 

---===---
Dataset (Downloaded from RoboFlow)
-
424 labelled 
images
-
1+ balls 
labelled per 
image
-
Court: 
wooden 
ground, 
white walls

---===---
Canny Edge Filter
-
Optimize model 
with Canny Edge 
Filter
-
Remove 
“obviously” 
incorrect edges 
(i.e. straight 
lines)

---===---
Model
- Used an Encoder-Decoder 
Model (w/ downsampling, then 
upsampling)
- Generated a heatmap
- Loss Function: Modified 
Focal Loss
- Better to predict false 
positives than false 
negatives
- Parameters: Alpha, Beta, 
Ball Position weight

---===---
Kalman Filter
-
A Kalman Filter smooths noisy detections from model
-
Maintains an accurate ball position even when individual 
frames have poor detections, motion blur, or partial 
occlusions.
