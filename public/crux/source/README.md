# CRUX

An experimental indoor bouldering photo app with on-device hold detection, hold-shaped highlights, custom problems, and provisional difficulty estimates.

This portfolio preview is available at [jetpackjules.github.io/crux/](https://jetpackjules.github.io/crux/). It has no portfolio menu entry and asks search engines not to index its HTML, but anyone with the URL can access it. Automatic wall-angle inference is paused in this preview. Planning and grades use a vertical-wall assumption (0 degrees), not a measured wall angle. The retained surface code and research below describe the experimental implementation, not an active preview feature.

Serve the portfolio's `public/crux` folder over HTTPS or localhost. No app build, API key, cloud inference service, database or GPU server is required. Camera capture requires permission. YOLO detects holds using single-threaded ONNX Runtime Web in a worker. Local contour extraction and contact planning run in separate workers. The surface model is not loaded while automatic wall angles are paused.

## Problems and interaction

- A problem has an allowed handhold set, extra feet-only holds, two start hand contacts, two start foot contacts and a finish to match. Two hands, two feet, or a hand and foot may share a visibly large enough hold under separate capacity checks (at most two contacts per hold). Handholds also permit feet. Other holds are off.
- The default view highlights detected hold contours and dims the rest of the photo. All detected holds exposes the whole scan; initial detections briefly appear before unused outlines fade. Regeneration preserves the gray background. Contact labels use collision-aware placement and leader lines, and no caption obscures the wall. It does not impose a numbered hand sequence. Optional Climb preview shows one candidate four-limb sequence.
- Color mode includes every detected hold of the selected paint group, for both hands and feet. Each hold belongs to one group. Robust silhouette samples, local background rejection and perceptual color/lightness clustering distinguish similar paints on each wall instead of using a fixed list of broad color buckets. It proposes starts and the highest selected finish; it does not detect gym tape or separate disconnected problems that share a paint.
- Target, style, height and optional hold corrections regenerate automatically. Shuffle asks for alternatives. Three visible demo tabs switch walls. Photo uploads detect, outline and plan automatically using the vertical-wall assumption.
- Color, apparent size and visible shape are inferred. Grip depth remains unknown. Normal hold clicks do not open settings; Edit holds exposes optional corrections, including two separate start handholds or a matched start, finish, exclusion and grip override.
- The planner scores the result independently of the requested grade. If no close candidate exists, the UI says so instead of relabeling it with the target.
- The central photo estimate is the main grade in color mode, custom problem cards and PNG exports; a smaller rough range sits underneath. Automatic photos currently use a fixed ±3-grade allowance, clipped to V0–V12 (±2 for complete grip overrides and a candidate beta). This is not a calibrated confidence interval. The numerical grading rule is unchanged by this presentation update. Search can favor a short beta with longer reaches over an easier sequence.
- Scans show real results as they arrive: candidate locations, batches of hold contours and then the planned climb. Automatic wall fitting and the wall-angle overlay are paused in this preview. Replay reveal is an explicit timed replay of existing results. Reduced motion disables the sweep and fades without delaying results.

## Planning and limits

The planner searches left/right hand and foot contacts, checks approximate reach and a body-position/support proxy during individual limb moves, then rechecks the allowed set for easier shortcuts. The default body model is 1.75 m tall. The current planner accepts heights from 120 to 220 cm; values outside that range are rejected before route search. Climber height sets hand reach proportionally (1.10 m at 1.70 m height) and changes stance checks and separately labeled personal effort. Conventional grade uses a fixed 1.70 m reference and 1.10 m reach. Dimensions & reach is collapsed by default and exposes only wall height, climber height and optional top/mat guides; image dimensions and reach remain internal. Search is bounded, not optimal.

These are two-dimensional, static geometry checks, not a physical simulation. This preview assumes a vertical wall (0 degrees) for all photos while automatic wall angles are paused. It does not measure or apply local wall inclines. Hold depth, friction, useful pulling direction, load capacity, foot technique, dynamics, flags, heel/toe hooks, smearing and volumes are not modeled. The match-size screen only rejects visibly undersized holds; it cannot establish usable two-hand grip. A candidate beta is not proof the climb is possible. Setter/climber forerunning remains necessary. Grade ranges are hand-authored and uncalibrated, not statistical confidence intervals.

Visible hold area is a weak grading cue, not a jug/crimp classifier. Large slopers may be poor and small positive holds useful. Contours can still miss surfaces or merge shadows. A bounded seeded-color retry expands clipped/uncertain outlines beyond the detector box, excludes neighboring detector boxes, and preserves existing strong outlines. Detector anchors remain fixed; recovered silhouette bounds supply visible extents. Uncertain extraction produces a dashed, unfilled bounding box. No silhouette accuracy benchmark was measured.

This remains a still-photo planner, not live AR. Natural rock is unsupported. Grade checks stay in localStorage; they measure your observations but do not train or calibrate the app. Photos are not retained after reload.

## Retained wall-geometry research — paused in this preview

The following pipeline and its angle-specific controls are retained as research code. They do not run in this portfolio preview, which assumes a vertical wall and keeps the climber's facet orientation neutral.

All photos now use the same automatic image-and-normal pipeline. Metric3D ViT Small predicts surface normals at preserved image proportions. A local seam detector joins aligned image edges into straight segments; these partition the wall into polygonal cells. Coplanar joints merge only when local normals agree, while supported creases veto even transitive merges. Faces with similar rounded inclines can remain distinct when their normals turn in different directions. Normal statistics exclude detected holds. Mixed cells retain their boundaries but withhold an angle, and unsupported normal-only changes do not create wavy seams. A second full-gradient pass runs only inside unresolved cells, accepting new edges with a supported normal change. Previously accepted regions and pixel assignments stay fixed. Refined cells require at least 85% agreeing normal samples before receiving an angle. The cave fixture recovers its left crease, but two mixed cells remain unresolved; the five accepted overhang cells stay unchanged. Mixed cells can retain hold-local slope estimates by resampling a coherent wall ring outside hold masks. The entire sampling window must fit inside one straight cell; a hold-only footprint check is insufficient. These estimates feed local grade and torso calculations without promoting the mixed cell to one plane. Mixed-cell labels show the span of supported local estimates. A single accepted vertical plane has a label without a tinted search-envelope outline. No demo identity, hand-traced polygon or photo-specific angle is used. The hold envelope and selected area only limit computation; region extents are approximate and are not surveyed physical boundaries.

Each accepted face receives an approximate angle from its normal and the likely floor normal. If the floor cannot be identified, a labeled upright-camera assumption supplies the reference. Angles are rounded to 5 degrees. The secondary ranges describe heuristic angular dispersion, not measured accuracy or calibrated confidence. A convincing-looking model error can pass these checks, especially in dark caves or around large volumes. No measured ground truth is available for these photos; this release cannot promise precise physical angles.

Per-hold assignment checks the visible footprint against region labels. Holds spanning seams remain unresolved. The climber uses supporting normals and polygon membership, with straight seam cells. No manual wall-angle override is exposed. Geometry is cached for area changes and recomputed for a new photo.

In the research implementation, the image top-left has one prioritized progress indicator for hold detection, wall detection and route updates. Wall detection finishes with a face count that fades after 2.6 seconds. Opening Wall angles rechecks cached geometry without rerunning the neural model or regenerating an unchanged route. These wall-detection steps and controls are paused in the portfolio preview. Help and estimation dialogs are not exposed; research and rules stay in this repository.

The live scan has no minimum phase duration. Contour previews stream in batches of eight; final relative sizes and paint groups are still calculated across all holds. New scans and page suspension cancel the contour worker and reject stale results. This preview proceeds to planning with its vertical-wall assumption without waiting for surface inference. Image controls share one row. Wheel zoom, mouse drag and phone pinch replace the zoom buttons; double-click background or press 0 to fit.

Limit area opens a movable rectangle with eight touch-sized resize handles. Included holds highlight live; Apply area commits it. Arrow keys move or resize the focused control, with Shift for larger steps. The full photo remains available for floor evidence. New photos suggest top/mat guides and assume 4 m between them; the cropped original demo uses a 3 m visible-height assumption. Width follows aspect ratio. These dimensions are assumptions, not measurements.

## Models, source and rules

The detector is a 12.3 MB fixed-960 adaptation of `jwlarocque/yolov8n-freeclimbs-detect-2`, with learned weights unchanged. The retained surface model is the unchanged publisher FP16 Metric3D ViT Small ONNX export, 75.8 MB split into four static files; it is not loaded or run while wall angles are paused. Model and runtime files are served from this portfolio app's own folder. The older depth-fitting implementation is retained for regression research and is also inactive. Fonts use Google Fonts. No photo is sent to an external AI provider.

Application source is AGPL-3.0-or-later. The app's `source.html`, `source/`, and `licenses/` provide code, reproducible model conversion and notices. [Portfolio preview source](https://github.com/Jetpackjules/Jetpackjules.github.io/tree/main/public/crux) includes this deployment's changes; the [original CRUX repository](https://github.com/Jetpackjules/crux-wall-lab) retains the development project. Research links are retained below. The original photo is Miyuki Meinaka, CC BY-SA 4.0. The additional two photos were supplied by the owner and are not covered by the source license. This preview is publicly accessible by direct URL and is omitted from the portfolio menu.

The research links cover World Climbing 2026 rules, USA Climbing setting guidance, BMC route reading, REI hold technique and differing gym volume policies. Four competition start markings can share holds; a controlled two-hand finish is required. CRUX explicitly proposes its own contacts and does not verify existing start tape or local volume/wall rules. Research models for segmentation and limited grade classification were reviewed; no new model was trained or claimed to generalize to arbitrary gym grades.

## Verification

- Planner assertions run against the actual shipped module: all contacts allowed, exclusions honored, supported starts/finishes, hand reach bounds, insufficient feet/disconnected failures, deterministic generation and target-independent scoring.
- Real detected holds from all three demos were checked at their assumed scales and 0, 30 and 65 degrees. Returned sequences validated within this model; the angle sweep confirmed its stated geometry limitation.
- Contour synthetic checks cover concavity, uniform-color fallback, image boundaries and malformed inputs; outlines were visually reviewed on the three photos.
- Palette regression matches an independent visual review of all 89 detected straight-wall holds: dark rose 17, flamingo pink 11, ice blue 8, slate blue 12, blue violet 11, green 9, yellow 11 and orange 10. Tests also preserve all 176 cave and 198 overhang detections, exclusive membership and contours. This is not a general color accuracy benchmark; uneven lighting on faceted walls can still split one paint into multiple groups. Runtime grouping uses the photo, never these review labels or a fixed number of colors.
- Browser checks cover automatic updates, demo switching, color mode, optional beta, hidden-by-default editor, PNG preview and responsive layout. Physical climbing validity and phone camera performance still require field testing.

## Mobile web app

The app has a manifest, home-screen icons and an install/help action. A service worker caches only same-origin static application/model assets; uploaded/camera photos never enter its cache. Cache names and cleanup are isolated to the app's URL path, preserving other CRUX copies on the same origin. Initial access and uncached assets require internet, and offline availability depends on browser storage. Model assets are cached after use; the paused surface model is not requested. Local photos are still discarded on reload. Optional height persists locally.

Photo, contours and contact labels share one transform for 1–4x wheel/pinch zoom and panning. Browser page zoom remains enabled outside the wall. One-finger movement at fit size scrolls the page. On a zoomed wall it pans the image. Gesture movement suppresses accidental hold clicks. Climb preview names the moving limb, shows a move arrow, and can play automatically.

V4 regression evidence: the reported yellow hold now includes its upper fin; three surrounding wall witnesses and 88 neighboring hold centers are excluded. Synthetic multitone/chalk recovery and gesture/label tests pass. Synthetic two-face depth returns distinct 20° and 0° local slopes and leaves the joining seam unknown. These are implementation checks, not general segmentation, grade, biomechanical or angle accuracy claims. Real phone pinch/camera/offline behavior still needs field testing.

## Animated climber preview

Opening Climb preview starts playback automatically, with Show climber enabled by default. The toggle can hide the figure while keeping contact playback. A rear-view vector cutout uses two-bone arm/leg IK in wall meters, the actual beta body height (default 1.75 m), the planner hip positions, and the same contact offsets as planning. The fixed reference height used for grading does not set avatar scale. Wall dimensions still determine photo scale and perspective is not corrected.

Only the moving contact travels between holds; the other three endpoints remain fixed while the hips shift and joints are re-solved. Shared hands coincide, while shared feet and mixed contacts use the planner offsets. Adjacent steps animate for 850 ms; route changes snap to their own pose. Reduced motion skips interpolation. Pause settles the current step, hiding preview removes the figure, and the layer follows the photo zoom without intercepting gestures. The figure illustrates an existing candidate beta: joint limits, 3D motion, forces and collisions are not modeled. PNG problem export remains a hold-set diagram.

IK verification covers 78 generated poses across 120/170/220 cm proportionally scaled fixtures, 70 transitions with stationary contacts, fixed segment lengths within the planner numerical tolerance, shared contacts, missing hold references and zero-distance endpoints.

## Facet orientation and photo scale

The portfolio preview keeps the avatar's facet orientation neutral while wall angles are paused. The retained research implementation projects separate shoulder and hip frames from compatible contact normals and the floor direction. Full-photo crop mapping and a camera-ray differential supply bounded lean, narrowing and foreshortening. Signed turning changes the side shading and torso seam. Conflicting contact normals are rejected rather than averaged; a body-area patch can fill missing, but not conflicting, evidence. Unknown geometry retains a neutral frame. The projection blends toward neutral if needed to keep limb reach. This is an illustration, not an estimate of a human's actual pose, 3D reconstruction or physics.

All image geometry now uses one pixels-per-meter scale. The wall-span helper searches for color edges above/below holds in several image strips, excluding hold boxes, then proposes median top/mat rows. It can mistake wall seams for boundaries and falls back to an explicitly uncertain hold envelope. The guides are inspectable and adjustable; they are not a wall-segmentation result. Cropped walls and concealed mats need correction. No physical height is detected.

Auto scale assumes 4 m between guides and computes full-photo height = assumed wall height / normalized guide span. The cropped original demo starts in Manual to avoid claiming its clipped top is detected. Walltopia's commercial guidance spans 3–4.5 m, so 4 m is a product assumption, not a standard or measurement: https://walltopia.com/products/boulder-walls/ . Structure height, highest graspable point, mat level and wall-surface length differ, especially on roofs.

Validation covers front-on, 20-degree overhang and 40-degree side-facing projection, opposite signed turns, conflicting and missing normals, unchanged contact endpoints, linked photo dimensions, synthetic boundary detection and blank-image abstention. On the cave photo the first edge suggestion was 7–77% of the image: an assumed 4 m wall span gives roughly 5.714 m full-photo height and 10.159 m full-photo width. This is an assumed global scale, not calibrated perspective or measured dimensions.

## Adaptive outline recovery

Rejected silhouettes now get a crop with up to 192 samples per side (previously 112), background samples that exclude neighboring detections, and a bounded threshold sweep (1, .85, .70, .55). Recovery requires adjacent-threshold mask IoU >= .82, centered area and extents, limited context growth, and exclusion of neighboring hold centers. Each context stops at its first stable pair; accepted original contours are unchanged. Recovery scores remain heuristic and capped at .65. The detector threshold is unchanged. No new model or server is used.

On the retained offline photo fixtures, fallback counts changed from 1 to 0 (straight), 36 to 13 (cave), and 14 to 3 (overhang). All 412 accepted original polygons remain identical; four large pale teal overhang holds now recover. A low-contrast synthetic ellipse recovered at 0.992 mask IoU, while uniform and gradient backgrounds stay unresolved. These counts describe contour acceptance, not verified hold-detection accuracy: false detector proposals, shadows, cropped surfaces, and overlapping holds can still produce partial or incorrect contours. Real close-ups were reviewed; there is no general labeled silhouette benchmark.

In the retained research implementation, the climber illustration projects its torso onto the estimated local facet. This facet projection is inactive in the portfolio preview. Steep front-facing planes foreshorten the torso (45° gives about 71% projected length); camera pitch and yaw affect this projection. The visual hips are refit around fixed hand/foot contacts instead of undoing the tilt. Nearly edge-on roofs retain a small readable torso thickness. Missing/conflicting facet evidence stays neutral, and the no-floor fallback assumes an upright camera. Incompatible projected reach is labeled as a stretched illustration; this does not change route grades or prove feasibility.

Automatic surface validation: synthetic maps recover separate 0/45-degree faces and keep seam-crossing holds unresolved. Raster hole rings remain unfilled; image-supported seams, approximate normal boundaries and computation-limit edges carry separate metadata. The renderer omits computation-limit strokes. On retained native FP32 normal fixtures, the JavaScript extractor assigned 117/176 cave and 166/198 overhang hold footprints. This is coverage, not correctness: the cave main roof crease is still missed and some lower surfaces merge. The production FP16 model was checked against FP32 numerically and in the desktop browser. Higher input resolution changed estimates substantially without consistently improving visible boundaries, so the smaller input is retained. These tests do not establish angle accuracy or physical climbing validity.
